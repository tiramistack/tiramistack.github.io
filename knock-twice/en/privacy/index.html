This page explains what data the app uses, why, who sees it and how you can delete it. We have tried to keep it simple. If something is unclear, write to us: tiramistack@gmail.com.

In short

  • Knock Twice is a test version (alpha) for invited people in Italy.
  • To use the app you need an email address, a name for your door plate and your date of birth. You must be at least 18.
  • Your library, reviews, quotes and journal pages are private: nobody else can see them in the app.
  • No ads, no statistics on what you do in the app, no newsletter. We do not sell your data.
  • On Android, only if you choose to, the app sends us technical reports when it crashes, through Google's Firebase Crashlytics. It stays off until you say yes, and you can turn it off at any time (section 14).
  • The reading calendar keeps times, plans and reminders only on your phone. You can choose to also save the day, duration and book of your sessions online: it is optional and you can change your mind at any time (section 13).
  • You can delete your account in the app, from Profile → "Delete account": deletion is immediate. You can also ask by email, without the app: we usually do it within 7 days, and at the latest within one month (section 10).
  • You can download a copy of your data in the app, from Profile → "Download my data", free of charge and whenever you like. You can also ask for it by email (section 9).
  • When you remove a book or a review, it disappears from your app and we also erase its content from our database. Only an empty marker remains, which keeps your devices in step, until you delete your account. We explain this below.

2. What data we use

Data you give us

  • Email address: to sign in with the 6-digit code.
  • Name on your door plate: whatever you type. You can use a nickname.
  • Date of birth: to check that you are at least 18 (see section 4). We ask for it before your email.
  • App language.
  • Your library: books, reading status, progress, dates, rereads, format, where the book came from, price if you enter it, moods, genres and tropes, shelves.
  • What you write: ratings, reviews and impressions, quotes, favorite characters.
  • Your book searches: the title, author or ISBN you search for. We do not keep a search history (section 5).
  • Covers you create: the cover "recipe", meaning the template, colors, stickers and text you choose.
  • Your reading journal, if you use it: one page per book, saved as a "recipe" (paper, stickers, tape, stamps, where each element sits, and the writing you add yourself). Your rating, impressions, quote and dates are not copied into the page: the page shows them by reading your review, quotes and book, and if you delete them they disappear from the page too. The journal holds no photos.
  • The reading calendar, if you use it: your reading sessions, plans and reminders. What stays on your phone and what reaches us is explained in section 13.
  • Messages you send us by email.

Data created while you use the app

  • Your acceptance of the Terms: which version and when.
  • Technical access data: account identifier, creation and last sign-in dates, IP address and basic device information, in the technical logs of our provider (Supabase).
  • If you turn on crash reports: technical data about app crashes and about your phone, with random identifiers for this installation (section 14).

Be careful with what you write. The books you read, your ratings and your notes can reveal very personal things, such as health, religion, political views or sex life. Only enter what you want to. We do not use your content to work out who you are: we keep it only to show it back to you.

Camera. If you use the scanner, the camera reads the book barcode. The image stays on your phone: it does not reach us and we do not save it. You can refuse the permission and add the book by hand.

What we do not collect. We do not use your location, contacts or photos. We do not upload your images. We do not use advertising tools or tools that analyze your behavior. The only outside diagnostic tool is Firebase Crashlytics, and only if you turn it on (section 14).

4. Minimum age: 18

  • Knock Twice is only for people aged 18 and over. Before asking for your email, we ask for your date of birth. If it shows you are under 18, you cannot get in and we receive nothing: neither the date nor the email. Your phone only keeps, for 7 days, a note that the door is closed, without the date.
  • If an account was created anyway, you can delete it yourself in the app with "Delete my data"; otherwise we delete it ourselves: if the email address was never confirmed, 7 days after it was created; if it was confirmed but no date of birth was saved and the account holds no book or other content, after 30 days without activity.
  • If we find out that a user is under 18, we close the account and delete the data (section 10). If you are a parent and think your child has signed up, write to us and we will delete everything.
  • The check is based on what you declare: we do not ask for ID. Please enter your real date of birth.

Your date of birth is never shown to other people. Once saved, you cannot change it yourself in the app. If you made a mistake, write to us and we will correct it. If the corrected date shows you are under 18, we delete the account.

5. Who can see your data

Other people using the app: nobody. This version has no public profiles, friends, comments or sharing.

Us: the controller can access the database to run the service, fix problems and answer your requests. We do not read your content unless that is needed for those reasons.

Providers working for us (processors, bound by a contract):

Provider What it does Where
Supabase, Inc.Database, sign-in, server functionsServers in Frankfurt (Germany). See section 6
Brevo SAS (Paris, France)Sends the emails with your code and the account deletion confirmation: it receives your address and the email text, which does not contain your name or your books. The emails contain a Brevo pixel and links used only for anonymous statistics (section 8)Servers in the European Union. See section 6
Google Ireland Limited (Firebase Crashlytics)Receives crash reports, only if you turn them on (section 14). Contract: Firebase Data Processing and Security TermsGoogle servers around the world, including the United States. See section 6

Outside services acting on their own account (independent controllers):

  • GitHub (GitHub B.V., Netherlands, and GitHub, Inc., United States): hosts the pages of this policy, the Terms and account deletion (GitHub Pages). When you open them, GitHub logs your IP address for security purposes, under its own privacy statement. The pages use no cookies, scripts or statistics.
  • Internet Archive / Open Library (United States): when the app shows a catalog cover, including in search results, your phone downloads it directly from their servers. They therefore receive your IP address and know which cover was requested: from the covers in your results they could guess what you are searching for. They do not receive your name, email or account. Downloaded covers stay in local storage on your phone. Open Library also receives your searches, but from our server: see below.
  • Google (Android only): the app contains the Google ML Kit tool for reading barcodes. Reading happens on your phone and images do not go to Google. ML Kit does, however, send Google technical data about how it works: device model, operating system, app version, identifiers tied to the installation, performance and errors. According to Google, these identifiers are not meant to recognize a person. This tool is not used on iPhone.

Searching for books. When you search for a book, your phone sends the search to our server, which looks it up in the app's shared catalog and asks Open Library (Internet Archive, United States):

  • by title or author: Open Library receives the text you typed and the app's language;
  • by ISBN, including with the scanner: Open Library receives only the ISBN, and only if the book is not already in the app's catalog.

The request comes from our server: Open Library sees the server's address and the contact email of the app's developer, which Open Library asks its users to provide. It does not receive your IP address, name, email or account. If you type information about yourself in the search box, it receives that too, so only type titles, authors or ISBNs.

No search history. We do not keep a history of your searches, on our server or on your phone (your phone only keeps the images of covers already shown, including in results: section 8), and our server logs do not record the text you search for. Only one trace remains: to speed up identical searches, we keep the search text (in lowercase, without accents or punctuation) together with Open Library's results for 24 hours, with no link to you. After 24 hours we stop using it, and it is deleted automatically at the next new search made by anyone using the app.

To prevent abuse, our server counts how many searches each account makes in one minute. It does this only in its memory, without the search text.

When you pick a book that was not in the catalog yet, its record goes into the app's shared catalog: only facts about the book (title, authors, publisher, year, pages, language, ISBN and cover address), with no link to your name. New records come from Open Library: we do not use Google Books.

Authorities: we give data to authorities only if the law requires us to.

We do not sell your data and we do not give it to anyone for advertising.

6. Data outside the European Union

Your data sits on servers in Frankfurt. It may leave the European Union in these cases:

  • Supabase is a US company. Its support staff and some of its providers, in the United States or Singapore, may process technical data or access data when support is needed or for security incidents. The safeguard is the standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission.
  • Brevo may use providers outside the Union. The safeguards are those set out in its data processing agreement, including the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • GitHub is a US company. For data leaving the Union it relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and the standard contractual clauses (Decision 2021/914), as stated in its privacy statement.
  • Google receives crash reports, if you turn them on (section 14), and may process them in the United States and other countries. The safeguard is the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, under which Google LLC is certified; if it were not available, the contract provides for the standard contractual clauses.
  • Internet Archive and Google receive the data described in section 5 in the United States.

You can ask us for a copy of the safeguards by writing to tiramistack@gmail.com.

8. Data on your phone

The app stores your library, content and preferences on your phone so that it works offline. It also stores your session, so you do not have to sign in every time. If your date of birth shows you are under 18, your phone stores only the moment of the block, for 7 days, so the question is not asked again right away, and then deletes it. The app does not encrypt this data: protect your phone with a screen lock.

When you sign out, the app deletes your library, reviews, quotes, shelves, journal pages, calendar sessions and plans, pending changes and profile from the phone, and removes scheduled reminders. The only exception is a deletion of the calendar's online copy that is still waiting for a connection: it stays recorded on the phone so it can be completed the next time you sign in (section 13). Only the catalog book records already downloaded, cover images, app preferences and the record of your acceptance of the Terms stay on the phone. To remove those too, uninstall the app.

When you delete your account in the app, the phone is also cleared of what signing out leaves behind: the calendar deletion still waiting to be sent, the record of your acceptance of the Terms and the cover images. The app also deletes the catalog book records already downloaded and your library view preferences. Only the device preferences stay: language, day or night, audio and room. On your other phones, the app signs out and deletes your data the next time it connects to the internet.

The app does not take part in Android backup or in transferring data to a new phone: its data is not copied to your Google account. The alpha is available on Android only.

The copy of your data. With "Download my data" (section 9) the app prepares the files on your phone, in a temporary folder reserved for the app, and opens your phone's share menu. The files do not go through our server and we do not see them. The app deletes them when you close the menu or, if the app closes first, the next time it opens. On Android, the copy handed to the app you picked stays in the same reserved folder until you next open Knock Twice. Where you save the file, or who you send it to, is up to you: from then on that copy is outside our control and follows the rules of the service you chose, for example your email or your cloud storage. The file contains personal data, such as your date of birth and what you have written: keep it safe.

All this data is needed to run the app you asked to use, so we do not ask for separate consent (Art. 122 of the Italian Privacy Code). The one exception is crash reporting (section 14): it starts and stores its data on your phone only if you turn it on. The app does not use cookies or tracking tools. Service emails, meaning those with your sign-in code and the deletion confirmation, are sent through Brevo and contain an invisible pixel and links used only for anonymous, aggregated statistics, not linked to you.

9. Your rights

You can ask us at any time to:

  • see your data and get a copy;
  • correct it, including your date of birth;
  • delete it (to delete your account, see section 10);
  • restrict how we use it;
  • receive it in a machine-readable format, to take it elsewhere;
  • object to what we do on the basis of legitimate interest;
  • withdraw your consent, if you gave it.

Write to tiramistack@gmail.com from the email address of your account, so we know it is you. It is free. We reply within one month. If the request is complicated we may need longer, but we will tell you within the first month.

A copy of your data, in the app. In your Profile tap "Download my data". It is free and you can do it as often as you like. If you are online, the app first syncs with our server, so the file also contains what is saved online. You get two files:

  • a JSON file, readable by a computer and in a text editor, with a legend inside in Italian and English that explains codes and ratings;
  • the list of your books as a CSV file, to open in a spreadsheet.

The JSON file contains: your account (email, creation and last sign-in dates); your profile (name on your door plate, date of birth, language, who sees your library, version and date of the Terms you accepted, calendar consent with its dates); your books and library, with the covers you created; reviews, quotes and shelves; your journal pages; the reading calendar (section 13); the empty markers of what you removed (section 7), with no content. It also contains the changes your phone has not sent yet.

It does not contain: the technical logs of our providers (sign-ins, email delivery), backups, the emails we exchanged, device preferences (such as audio, day or night and room) and data kept only on other phones: the file contains what is on the phone you download it from. If you are offline or the server does not answer, the file contains only what is on the phone and says so ("complete": false, with the notices in "warnings").

A copy by email. You can also ask for it by writing to tiramistack@gmail.com from your account email address, even if you no longer have the app. We send a JSON file with the data we hold on the server, only to that address, usually within 7 days and at the latest within one month. We do not have the data kept only on your phone, such as calendar times and plans: you can get those only from the app. If you also want the technical logs, ask for them in the same email: we will give you those that still exist (section 7).

If you think we have mishandled your data, you can complain to the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (garanteprivacy.it), or to the authority of the country where you live.

10. How to delete your account

Signing out does not delete your account. You can delete it in two ways, free of charge and without giving a reason.

In the app

  1. Open your Profile and tap "Delete account".
  2. Read what will be deleted and what stays.
  3. Enter the 6-digit code we email you, so we know it is really you.
  4. Confirm with "Delete for good".

Deletion is immediate and cannot be undone: there is no waiting period. You need an internet connection. You can do it even if you have not finished signing up, or if the app did not let you in because of your age: in that case the option is called "Delete my data". Right after, we send you a confirmation email, without your name or any book titles. If you did not ask for the deletion, write to us right away at tiramistack@gmail.com. If you later sign in again with the same email, you will find a new, empty account.

Changes your phone has not sent yet are lost. Before deleting you can download a copy of your data: the first step has a "Download my data" link and, once sharing is done, you are back on the same step (section 9). The copy also contains the changes not sent yet and the data kept only on that phone. After deletion we can no longer give you a copy.

Without the app, by email

The same instructions are on the page https://tiramistack.github.io/knock-twice/en/delete-account/.

  1. Write to tiramistack@gmail.com from your account email address, with the subject "Delete my account".
  2. If you write from a different address, we send a code to the account address and ask you to tell us what it is. We do not ask for identity documents.
  3. We delete the account usually within 7 days and at the latest within one month of receiving your request, and confirm by email.

What we delete

Your account and email address, profile, the name on your door plate, date of birth, library, reviews, quotes, shelves, journal pages, covers and books you added by hand, including the empty markers of things you had removed (section 7); the online copy of your calendar sessions and the proof of your consent (section 13). If you delete in the app, also the data on that phone (section 8).

What stays, and for how long

  • books in the shared catalog, which contain no data about you;
  • Supabase technical logs, until they expire (about 1 day on our current plan);
  • Brevo email sending logs, until they expire (section 7);
  • backups, if any, until they expire (section 7);
  • the deletion register, for the time given in section 7;
  • the emails we exchanged, for the time given in section 7;
  • data on your other phones, until the app connects again (section 8);
  • crash reports already sent, until they expire after 90 days (section 14): they are not linked to your account, so we cannot find them to delete them sooner.

13. The reading calendar

The calendar is optional. It lets you time your reading with a timer (Start and Stop), add a past session by hand, plan your next sessions and get a reminder.

What data it uses

  • Reading sessions: day, duration in minutes and book. On the phone also the start and end time and the pages you started from and reached.
  • Planned sessions: book (if you pick one), days, time, repeat and reminder.
  • Your choices: where to save your sessions, reminders on or off, whether we have already asked for notification permission.
  • The page you enter when you stop the timer (at Stop), if you enter it: it updates the book's progress in your library.

Where it stays

Data Where
Start and end time, pages of each session, planned sessions, reminders and choicesOnly on the phone, always
Day, duration and book of your sessionsOn the phone. Also on our server only if you choose "Also online"
Page entered at Stop, meaning the book's progressOn our server with the rest of your library, even if you choose "Phone only"
Proof of consentOn our server, if you chose "Also online"

The first time you open the calendar we ask where to save your sessions, with two equal choices: "Phone only" or "Also online". All calendar features stay available with either choice.

With "Phone only" your sessions do not reach us. If you sign out, uninstall the app or switch phones, they are lost and we cannot recover them. Before that you can save a copy with "Download my data" (section 9).

With "Also online" we save a copy of the day, duration and book of each session, so you can find them on another phone or when you sign back in. We use it only for this, and no other app user can see it. It covers sessions saved after your choice that have a book: earlier sessions and sessions without a book stay on the phone.

If you edit a session, the change goes online only if that session is already online or was saved after your choice: book, day and duration, with the same delayed sending. A session that stayed on the phone stays there after the edit too. The session's times and pages stay on the phone, and the edit does not change the book's progress in your library.

Delayed sending. The online copy of a session, and the page entered at Stop, leave the phone no earlier than the next day, with a delay of a few hours that varies. If you sign out before then, they are sent at that moment. Our server records when it receives and changes each copy: because of the delay, that time usually does not match when you read.

Legal basis. For the online copy we rely on your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) and Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), because the books you read can reveal personal things, such as health, religion, political views or sex life. Consent is optional: if you do not give it, the calendar works anyway. The page entered at Stop is part of your library and follows the rules in section 3. Data that stays only on the phone is needed to run the calendar you chose to use (section 8).

If your age is not on record yet

If your date of birth is not yet on record in your account, for example because your phone could not read your profile, the calendar plays it safe:

  • your sessions stay only on the phone: the app does not offer online saving and our server refuses it. The page entered at Stop still goes online with your library;
  • reminders start switched off, and we do not schedule any between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. On Android a notification may arrive a little late, so one set shortly before 10 p.m. may arrive just after.

Once your date of birth is on record, you will find the "Also online" option in the calendar menu. We do not send you invitations.

Reminders

  • Your phone schedules them. They do not go through outside notification services (from Google, Apple or Huawei) or through our server: we do not know when they arrive.
  • We ask for notification permission only when you turn a reminder on. If you refuse, the calendar works anyway.
  • The text is always neutral: "Reminder" and "You have some reading planned." (or "You have some reading planned in 10 minutes."). It never contains the book's title or genre or the name on your door plate. Android still shows the app name.
  • You can turn off a single reminder from its plan, or all of them from the calendar menu.

Proof of consent

When you choose "Also online" we record on the server the version of the text you saw (for example 2026-11-en) and the date. If you withdraw consent we also record the date you withdrew. This data does not contain your reading sessions. It is used to show that you gave consent (Art. 7(1) GDPR) and to defend ourselves in a dispute (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We keep it while you have the account. When you delete your account we delete it with everything else (section 10). If you give consent again, the new version and date replace the earlier ones. We also keep the text of each version of the consent sheet, which contains no data about you.

How to withdraw consent

  • In the calendar menu open "Where your reading is saved" and choose "Phone only and delete the online copy". It takes as many steps as giving consent. Withdrawing does not make what we did before unlawful.
  • With a connection: we delete the copy of all your sessions from the server, and online saving is turned off on all your devices. The sessions already on each phone stay.
  • Without a connection: the choice is saved on the phone and no more sessions leave it. The copy on the server is deleted the next time you open the app while online, with the same account. If you sign out first, it happens the next time you sign in.
  • If you uninstall the app or never sign back in before the deletion runs, the copy stays online until you delete your account or the alpha ends. In that case write to tiramistack@gmail.com and we will delete it, usually within 7 days and at the latest within one month.
  • If our server does not accept the deletion, the app tells you and tries again later on its own. If the notice doesn't go away, write to us and we will delete the copy.
  • If you choose "Phone only" on another phone, the online copy is deleted and saving is turned off on all your devices.

Deletion

  • If you delete a session or its book, once the change reaches the server we remove the book, day and duration from the online copy. An empty marker remains, which keeps your devices in step and which we delete no sooner than 30 days later. The same happens if you remove the book from a session that is online: the online copy is deleted and the session stays only on the phone. Library content also disappears from the server (section 7), but there the empty marker stays while you have an account.
  • If you sign out, the app deletes your sessions and plans from the phone and removes your reminders (section 8).
  • If you delete your account, in the app or by email, we delete the online copy and the proof of consent together with the account (section 10).

End of the alpha. The online copy stays at most until the end of the alpha. Before then we will tell you by email and in the app, at least 30 days in advance. In the copy of your data. 14. Crash reports

For now on Android only.

It stays off until you turn it on. After you sign up, we ask you once whether you want to send us reports when the app crashes, with two equal choices. You can change your mind at any time in Settings → "Crash reports". If you say no, the app works exactly the same. Until you say yes, the service does not start and stores nothing on your phone.

What reaches us, if you turn it on

  • When the app crashes or hits an error: where in the code it happened and what kind of error it was, the time, the app version, your phone model, the operating system and its version, memory and free storage, whether the phone is rooted, whether the app was in the foreground, the screen orientation and the state of the proximity sensor.
  • Each time you open the app: a session-start signal with phone model, operating system, connection type and time. It is used to work out how many people use the app without crashes.
  • Random identifiers created for this installation of the app. They are not your account or your phone number, and they change if you reinstall the app.
  • Your IP address, which Google receives with every connection.

What does not reach us. Your email, door-plate name, account identifier, date of birth, books, reviews, quotes, journal, searches or calendar sessions. Before sending, we strip from error messages any text that could contain such data. We do not use Google Analytics.

Who receives it. Google, through the Firebase Crashlytics service, as our processor (contract: Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms). Google also processes, on its own account, some technical data about the use of its services, such as your IP address, under its own privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy). We have asked that this data not be used for other Google services.

Where. On Google servers around the world, including the United States (section 6).

Legal basis. Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and Art. 122 of the Italian Privacy Code).

How long. Google keeps crash reports for 90 days, then deletes them from live systems and backups. On your phone we store your choice, the version of this text and the date, as proof of consent.

How to turn it off. In Settings → "Crash reports". From then on the app stops sending reports and deletes those still waiting on your phone; Google's service switches off completely the next time you start the app. Reports already sent stay until they expire after 90 days: they are not linked to your account, so we cannot tell which ones are yours. Withdrawing does not make what we did before unlawful. If you delete your account, the choice on that phone goes back to "no".
